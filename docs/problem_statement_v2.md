# Problem Statement v2

> **Counterfactual Conversation Emulation for Medical AI Agents**
> Author: Oliver Liu — last updated 2026-05-03
> Status: v2 — supersedes the home-file Phase-1 statement; adds path-specific
> effects, sensitivity parameterization, and conformal coverage targets.

---

## 1. Setting

We observe a dataset of historical clinical encounters

$$
\mathcal{D} = \big\{(x_i,\, a_i^{\mathrm{clin}},\, y_i)\big\}_{i=1}^{n},
$$

where for each encounter $i$:

- $x_i \in \mathcal{X}$ is the **patient pre-decision context** — chief
  complaint, HPI, PMH/PSH, medications, allergies, social and family history,
  ROS, and physical-exam findings. By construction $x_i$ contains **only**
  information available *before* the clinician committed to an assessment;
  the time-zero principle of target trial emulation requires this strictly
  (Hernán & Robins 2016, 2022).
- $a_i^{\mathrm{clin}} \in \mathcal{A}$ is the **clinician's actual final
  output** — assessment + plan, extracted from the SOAP note. The action
  space $\mathcal{A}$ is natural-language text (assessment + structured
  plan); we treat it as the agent's *final* output rather than the full
  multi-turn dialogue.
- $y_i \in [0, 1]^{K}$ is a **vector** of rubric scores along $K = 4$
  axes (see §3): DDx accuracy, Mx safety, Mx practicality, Mx cost. The
  scalar overall outcome is $\bar y_i = \tfrac{1}{K}\sum_k y_{i,k}$.

The behavior policy $\pi_b$ is the (unknown) clinician decision policy that
generated $a^{\mathrm{clin}}$; the target policy $\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}$ is a
medical AI agent we wish to evaluate without running a prospective trial.

## 2. Estimand

The primary estimand is the **deployment value** of the agent:

$$
V(\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p(x)}\left[\,
  \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_{\mathrm{agent}}(\cdot \mid x)}\big[\, Y(x, a)\,\big]
\right]
$$

and the **policy contrast** versus the clinician baseline:

$$
\Delta(\pi_{\mathrm{agent}},\, \pi_b) \;=\; V(\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}) - V(\pi_b).
$$

Here $Y(x, a)$ is the potential-outcome rubric score had action $a$ been
taken in context $x$. We observe $Y(x_i, a_i^{\mathrm{clin}})$ but never
$Y(x_i, a_i^{\mathrm{agent}})$ in $\mathcal{D}$ — that is the counterfactual
we need to emulate.

### 2.1 Path-specific decomposition (U4)

Because the scalar $V$ marginalizes over clinically meaningful axes, we also
report the **decomposed contrast**

$$
\Delta_{k,\,s} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x \in s}\left[\, \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_{\mathrm{agent}}(\cdot|x)}[Y_k(x,a)] - \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_b(\cdot|x)}[Y_k(x,a)] \,\right]
$$

for each axis $k \in \{\text{DDx},\text{Mx safety},\text{Mx practicality},\text{Mx cost}\}$
and each subpopulation $s$ defined by ICD chapter, age band, or comorbidity
count. This directly addresses the dominant gap reported in the AMIE-BIDMC
clinical study (Brodeur et al. 2026), where PCPs beat AMIE on practicality
($p = 0.003$) and cost-effectiveness ($p = 0.004$) — a scalar
$V(\pi_{\mathrm{agent}})$ would average that signal away.

## 3. Outcome model

$Y_k(x, a)$ is generated by a **multi-agent debate judge** (U2) with three
LLMs from three vendors (Anthropic / Google / OpenAI) playing defender,
prosecutor, and arbitrating judge. For each axis $k$ the judge outputs a
Beta posterior $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_k, \beta_k)$ over $[0, 1]$; we use the
posterior mean $\hat y_k = \alpha_k / (\alpha_k + \beta_k)$ as the point
estimate and the concentration $\alpha_k + \beta_k$ as a per-case outcome
uncertainty signal that feeds U1's nuisance estimation.

The four axes mirror the BIDMC rubric:

| $k$ | Axis | Interpretation |
|-----|------|---------------|
| 1 | DDx accuracy | Top-K differential includes the true diagnosis with appropriate ranking |
| 2 | Mx safety | Plan avoids contraindications; reflects patient allergies/comorbidities |
| 3 | Mx practicality | Plan is locally executable in primary care (no exotic referrals) |
| 4 | Mx cost | Plan is cost-aware (uses generics, defers low-yield imaging) |

## 4. Identification assumptions

For unbiased identification of $V(\pi_{\mathrm{agent}})$ from observational
$\mathcal{D}$ we require, at the time-zero per-encounter level (analogue of
Hernán & Robins 2022 §3):

| # | Assumption | Concrete meaning here |
|---|-----------|-----------------------|
| **A1** | **Consistency** | $Y_i = Y(x_i, a_i^{\mathrm{clin}})$ — observed score equals the potential outcome under the action that was actually taken |
| **A2** | **Positivity (overlap)** | $p_{\pi_b}(a \mid x) > 0$ wherever $\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}(a \mid x) > 0$ — every action the agent might take must have non-zero probability under the clinician |
| **A3** | **Exchangeability (no unmeasured confounding)** | $\{Y(x, a)\,\forall a\} \perp\!\!\!\perp A^{\mathrm{clin}} \mid X$ — given $x$, the clinician's choice is independent of potential outcomes |
| **A4** | **No anticipation** | The clinician's behavior in encounter $i$ is not affected by whether $\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}$ is later evaluated against it (trivially true here — historical data) |
| **A5** | **No interference** | Encounter $i$'s outcome does not depend on actions taken in encounter $j \neq i$ (panel-management edge cases violate this; we exclude them) |

**A2 is the most problematic** in a natural-language action space: the agent
may produce assessments the clinician would never write. We mitigate via the
MIPS marginalization (action embedded into $\phi(a) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with
$d \ll |\mathcal{A}|$, requiring overlap only in the embedding space), and we
diagnose violations explicitly (§5 positivity gate).

**A3 is heroic** in medicine: clinicians choose actions partly based on
unmeasured factors (social cues, patient demeanor, time pressure). We do
not pretend A3 holds — instead we report **bounded effects** under a marginal
sensitivity model (§6).

## 5. Estimators (U1 + U3)

We compare six estimators against ground truth $V^{\mathrm{true}}$ obtained
from a real prospective comparison on the same $x$ (held aside, never used
during estimation):

1. **DM** — Direct method: train $\hat f(x, a) \approx \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X=x, A=a]$
   on $\mathcal{D}$, predict $\hat V_{\text{DM}} = \tfrac1n \sum_i \hat f(x_i, a_i^{\mathrm{agent}})$.
2. **IPS (classifier-based DRE)** — embed $a$ via off-the-shelf $\phi$,
   train classifier $c$ to discriminate agent-sampled vs clinician-sampled
   $\phi(a)$, derive $w(x, a) = c(x, \phi(a))/(1 - c(\cdot))$. Self-normalize
   and 99-quantile truncate.
3. **Vanilla DR** — $\hat V_{\text{DR}} = \tfrac1n \sum_i \big[\hat f(x_i, a_i^{\mathrm{agent}}) + w_i\,(y_i - \hat f(x_i, a_i^{\mathrm{clin}}))\big]$.
4. **MIPS over CCE embedding (U3)** — replace off-the-shelf $\phi$ with a
   causally contrastive embedding (§ U3), use Saito & Joachims 2022's
   marginalized IPS estimator with cluster effect (OffCEM, Saito 2023).
5. **DML cross-fit DR (U1)** — 5-fold sample split for nuisance estimation
   to remove first-order bias (Chernozhukov et al. 2018).
6. **TMLE (U1)** — targeted update (van der Laan 2006) for semi-parametric
   efficiency.

For each estimator we report:

- **Point bias**: $\hat V - V^{\mathrm{true}}$
- **RMSE** over $B = 1000$ bootstrap resamples
- **CI coverage** under (a) BCa bootstrap CI and (b) **conformal CI** (Taufiq
  et al. 2022, NeurIPS) at nominal $1 - \alpha = 0.9$
- **Direction agreement** with $\mathrm{sign}(V^{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}) - V^{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_b))$

### 5.1 Positivity gate

Before running any IPS-type estimator we require both:

- $\mathrm{ESS} / n > 0.10$
- DRE classifier AUC $< 0.90$

If either fails on a given embedding × dataset combination, we report DM
and DR only and flag the failure mode in the per-case diagnostic.

## 6. Marginal Sensitivity Model (U4)

We adopt a **logistic marginal sensitivity model** (Tan 2006; Yadlowsky et al.
2018; Kallus & Zhou 2021) parameterized by $\Gamma \geq 1$:

$$
\frac{1}{\Gamma} \;\leq\; \frac{p(A = a \mid X = x,\, U)}{p(A = a \mid X = x)} \;\leq\; \Gamma
\quad \text{for all unmeasured } U,
$$

where $\Gamma = 1$ corresponds to A3 holding exactly, and larger $\Gamma$
encodes increasingly strong unmeasured confounding. For each estimator we
report the **worst-case bounds** $[\hat V^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{lo}},\, \hat V^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{hi}}]$
at $\Gamma \in \{1.0,\, 1.5,\, 2.0,\, 3.0\}$.

A primary headline claim — e.g. "$\pi_{\mathrm{agent}}$ improves $V$ vs the
clinician baseline" — must be **robust at $\Gamma = 2$** (a rule of thumb
in clinical OPE studies) to be reported as the main result; otherwise it is
flagged as "fragile under unmeasured confounding."

## 7. Conformal coverage target

Bootstrap CIs assume first-order normality of the estimator and undercover
in the heavy-tailed-weight regime typical of high-dimensional NLP IPS.
Following Taufiq et al. (2022) we additionally compute **split-conformal CIs**
that are finite-sample valid:

- Calibration target: $\mathbb{P}\big(V^{\mathrm{true}} \in \widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_{\alpha}\big) \geq 1 - \alpha$
- Nominal level: $1 - \alpha = 0.90$
- Empirical coverage threshold: an estimator passes if empirical coverage
  over $B$ resamples lies in $[0.85,\, 0.92]$.

## 9. Key risks (mapped to home-file Phase-7 risks)

| Risk | Trigger | Fallback |
|------|---------|----------|
| R1 — judge unreliable | calibration $\rho < 0.7$ | escalate to ensemble/debate; ultimately switch to gold-label diagnosis-accuracy task |
| R2 — positivity fails | ESS/n $< 0.05$ or AUC $> 0.95$ | restrict target population to overlap subset; report DM + DR only |
| R3 — no estimator recovers GT | Week-10 milestone fails | reframe as cautionary study (still publishable per home-file Risk 3) |
| R4 — sensitivity bound too wide | sign flips at $\Gamma = 1.2$ | report effect as "fragile"; still informative |
| R5 — conformal CI too wide | width $> 4\times$ point estimate | report split-conformal + bootstrap hybrid CI |

## 10. Success milestones

- **End of Week 5** — Ground truth constructed, true effect statistically
  significant at $\Gamma = 1$.
- **End of Week 10** — At least one estimator (TMLE or MIPS over CCE)
  achieves $|{\mathrm{bias}}| < 20\%$ of $V^{\mathrm{true}}$, direction
  agreement $> 90\%$, and conformal coverage $\in [0.85,\, 0.92]$.

If both milestones are met, paper is writable. If either fails, paper
re-positions per R3.
